  1. Case Report

    Modeling and Forecasting U.S. Federal Debt with a Log-ARIMA(4,1,4) Model

    STAT-GB 6018 Forecasting Time Series Data, NYU Stern · Prof. Avi Giloni Fall 2025

    Fits an ARIMA(4,1,4) model with a constant to the log of quarterly U.S. Federal Debt (FRED series GFDEBTN, 1966–2025) after a first difference removes the deterministic upward trend; AICc selects this specification over the no-constant ARIMA(5,1,5) alternative (-1334.01 vs. -1330.70). The AR(4) and MA(4) coefficients are highly significant (p < 0.001), consistent with shocks that stay influential for roughly four quarters — a near-annual cycle in quarterly data — and standardized residuals show no significant ACF/PACF spikes. Ljung-Box tests flag mild residual autocorrelation at the 3- and 6-year horizons (p = 0.001, 0.039) that fades by lag 36, pointing to a longer-run cyclical component the model doesn't fully absorb. A 50-quarter-ahead forecast tracks the historical trend closely, with 95% intervals that widen at a reasonable rate.

  2. Case Report

    Forecasting the USD/JPY Exchange Rate with an ARIMA(5,1,4)–GARCH(1,1) Model

    STAT-GB 6018 Forecasting Time Series Data, NYU Stern · Prof. Avi Giloni Fall 2025

    Models daily USD/JPY (Yahoo Finance, Dec 2020–Nov 2025) by taking logs to stabilize the trend, then differencing once after the PACF showed a near-unit root and a second difference introduced strong negative autocorrelation. An AICc grid search over ARIMA(p,1,q) selects ARIMA(5,1,4) with a constant (AICc = -9619.17); the residuals are serially uncorrelated but their squares are not, so an ARCH(q) sweep (q = 0–10) and a GARCH(1,1) alternative are compared, with GARCH(1,1) winning decisively (AICc = -9800.39 vs. -9701.83 for the best ARCH(4)). The fitted GARCH(1,1) (α̂ = 0.047, β̂ = 0.948, α̂+β̂ = 0.996) implies a stationary, highly persistent volatility process. Layering GARCH(1,1) onto the ARIMA(5,1,4) mean equation narrows the one-step-ahead 95% forecast interval for log(JPY) from [5.04145, 5.06480] to [5.044003, 5.062238], and a backtest against the |standardized residual| > 1.96 rule finds a 5.39% violation rate across 1,299 observations — close to the nominal 5%, though a normal probability plot of the residuals still shows tail deviations consistent with leptokurtosis the model doesn't fully capture.

  3. Case Report

    Charity Ratings and Accountability: A Correlation Analysis of CharityNavigator Data

    STAT-UB 17 Regression and Multivariate Data Analysis, NYU Stern · Prof. Sarah Abramowitz Spring 2026

    Uses CharityNavigator.org data to test how a charity's overall rating relates to its accountability and transparency score and to whether it publishes audited financial statements. Scatterplots show a clear positive linear pattern with the (continuous) accountability and transparency score and no notable outliers, while the (binary) audited-statements groups cluster at roughly 70–95 (no) versus 80–100 (yes) overall rating. Correlation tests find both relationships statistically significant (p < 2.2e-16 and p < 3.546e-16 respectively), with accountability and transparency the stronger predictor (r = 0.707, a strong correlation by Cohen's scale) versus audited-statement disclosure (r = 0.428, a moderate correlation) — the former also admits a directional linear interpretation that the binary variable does not.

  4. Case Report

    Unemployment and Labor Force Participation: A Structural Break Around the 2008 Financial Crisis

    STAT-UB 17 Regression and Multivariate Data Analysis, NYU Stern · Prof. Sarah Abramowitz Spring 2026

    Tests whether the civilian unemployment rate (CUNR) predicts labor force participation (CLFPR) using 1980–2024 FRED data, to weigh evidence for the discouraged-worker versus added-worker effect. Over the full sample the two variables show no linear pattern and no significant correlation (p ≈ 0.89), but splitting the data at the 2008 financial crisis reveals two opposite, individually significant regimes: 1980–2008 is strongly negative (r ≈ -0.79, p = 3.17e-07; CLFPR = 69.66 − 0.62·CUNR, R² ≈ 0.63), consistent with the discouraged-worker effect, while 2009–2024 is moderately positive (r ≈ 0.68, p ≈ 0.0027; CLFPR = 61.28 + 0.33·CUNR, R² ≈ 0.46), consistent with the added-worker effect. The opposing signs of similar magnitude explain why the pooled sample shows no relationship. Residual diagnostics flag 1980 and 1983 (via standardized residuals and Cook's distance, respectively) in the earlier period and 2009 and 2021 in the later one, illustrating how the two measures can flag different points depending on whether an observation has an extreme individual error or an extreme leverage value.

  5. Case Report

    Newspaper Advertisement Responses by Day and Section: A Two-Way ANOVA

    STAT-UB 17 Regression and Multivariate Data Analysis, NYU Stern · Prof. Sarah Abramowitz Spring 2026

    Uses a two-way ANOVA to test whether the number of responses to newspaper advertisements differs by day of week, by section (news, business, sports), and whether the two factors interact, after confirming skewness ratios (max 0.64) and Levene's test (p = 0.975) support the normality and equal-variance assumptions. The day×section interaction is highly significant (p < .001) with a large effect (partial η² ≈ 0.63): Business leads on Monday while News and Sports both peak on Friday, and the ranking of sections flips across the week. Tukey-adjusted simple-effects comparisons show Business beats News and Sports on Monday, News beats Sports on Tuesday and Wednesday, Business beats News on Thursday, and News beats Business on Friday; within sections, News dips sharply on Thursday and rebounds by Friday, Business peaks on Monday, and Sports peaks on Friday. The results argue against optimizing ad placement on a single factor — day and section have to be chosen jointly to maximize response.

  1. GROUP PROJECT

    StandardAero: A Take-Private LBO Case (VioletStone Capital)

    FINC-UB 32-002 Private Equity Finance, NYU Stern · Prof. Eliot Merrill Spring 2026

    A take-private LBO pitch for StandardAero (NYSE: SARO), the world's largest independent pure-play aerospace-engine-aftermarket provider, structured at an $11.7B entry TEV (14.5x 2025A Adj. EBITDA of $808M) with a 55%/45% equity/debt split and 6.5x leverage. The thesis rests on a $123B→$138B global MRO tailwind driving revenue growth through engine shop visits rather than new aircraft, and a margin-recovery path (13.3%→~14.5% Adj. EBITDA margin) as the LEAP learning curve matures and higher-margin Component Repair Services grow as a share of revenue. The base Investment Case models an 11.8% revenue CAGR to $10.6B by 2030 and projects a 19.4% IRR / 2.4x MOIC on $6.8B of sponsor equity, versus 22.6% IRR / 2.8x MOIC under a Management Case (full LEAP industrialization plus M&A) and 12.4% IRR / 1.8x MOIC under a Downside Case. A value-creation bridge attributes roughly 93% of gross value creation to EBITDA growth against 7% from net debt paydown at a flat exit multiple, with sensitivity tables flexing entry/exit multiples from 12.5x–16.5x.

    Group project with Kelvin Torto, Maxon Yin, Sanansh Garg, Xubing Lai.
